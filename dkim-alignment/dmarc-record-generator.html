How To Generate DMARC Records With
SPF And DKIM Alignment In Mind


Email authentication helps protect business communications from spoofing, phishing, and unauthorized use. SPF and DKIM verify aspects of a message’s authenticity, while DMARC uses those checks and domain alignment to determine how messages should be handled. Creating a properly configured DMARC record requires understanding how SPF, DKIM, and alignment work together. When configured correctly, these protocols can help improve deliverability, protect domain reputation, and reduce the risk of fraudulent messages reaching recipients.


Understanding DMARC, SPF, and DKIM Alignment


DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance) builds upon SPF and DKIM by checking whether authenticated domains align with the domain visible in the email's From address.


What SPF Alignment Means

SPF checks whether an email server is authorized to send mail for a domain. SPF alignment is met when the domain authenticated by SPF matches the domain in the visible From address. For example, if the From address shows example.com while SPF authenticates mail.example.com, whether the domains align depends on the DMARC mode: relaxed alignment allows organizational domains to match despite different subdomains, while strict alignment requires an exact match.




Understanding DKIM Alignment

DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM) uses a cryptographic signature to verify that an email hasn’t been altered and is associated with an authenticated domain. DMARC checks whether the domain in DKIM’s d= tag aligns with the visible From domain. Relaxed alignment allows a match at the organizational-domain level; strict alignment requires an exact match. DMARC passes when either SPF or DKIM passes and aligns with the From domain.


Steps to Generate a DMARC Record


Step 1: Verify Existing SPF and DKIM Records

Before creating a DMARC record, verify that SPF and DKIM are correctly configured for every legitimate email-sending service, including Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, marketing automation tools, CRM systems, and transactional email providers. Confirm that each service is authorized in your SPF record and that its outgoing messages carry valid DKIM signatures using your domain.


Step 2: Choose a DMARC Generation Tool

A DMARC record generator creates a DNS-ready record based on your chosen policy and reporting preferences. Tools such as AutoSPF, PowerDMARC, and EasyDMARC can help administrators manage email authentication and related DNS settings.

Key tags include:

  • `v=DMARC1`: Identifies the DMARC protocol version.

  • `p=none`: Monitors authentication without requesting that messages be rejected.

  • `rua`: Specifies the destination for aggregate reports.

  • `adkim`: Sets DKIM alignment.

  • `aspf`: Sets SPF alignment.

Example monitoring record:

`v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com; adkim=r; aspf=r`

Replace the example reporting address with a mailbox configured to receive DMARC reports.


Step 3: Configure Alignment Settings

If your organization uses multiple email platforms or subdomains, start with relaxed alignment. The adkim=r and aspf=r settings allow organizational domain matching. Consider strict alignment once your sending infrastructure consistently authenticates with the exact domain shown in the From address. Test legitimate email sources before enforcing strict alignment, since misconfiguration can cause authentication failures.





Publish and Validate Your DMARC Record


Add the Record to DNS

Publish the generated record as a TXT record at _dmarc.yourdomain.com, replacing yourdomain.com with your actual domain. Then use a DMARC lookup or validation tool to check that the record is syntactically correct and publicly accessible. DNS propagation may take time, depending on your provider and TTL settings.


Monitor Reports Before Enforcement

Begin with p=none and review aggregate reports to identify sending sources, alignment failures, and unauthorized email activity. Once legitimate services consistently pass authentication, gradually advance to p=quarantine and then p=reject. After each policy change, review reports and work with email vendors to resolve authentication issues.


Common DMARC Configuration Mistakes to Avoid


Passing SPF or DKIM alone doesn’t ensure DMARC compliance; the authenticated domain must also align with the visible From domain. Avoid publishing multiple DMARC records for one domain, and account for every third-party email sender. Before moving to strict enforcement, review authentication reports.
