Alternatives To SPF Flattening For
Managing The 10 DNS Lookup Limit


Sender Policy Framework (SPF) helps domain owners prevent unauthorized senders from sending email on their behalf. But SPF authentication allows no more than 10 DNS lookups. Exceeding that limit can cause a permanent error (PermError), which may affect email delivery and domain reputation.

SPF flattening can reduce DNS lookups, but it isn’t the only way to manage a complex SPF record. Understanding the available alternatives can help businesses avoid lookup errors, simplify DNS management, and support reliable email authentication and deliverability. Why Look for Alternatives to SPF Flattening?


SPF flattening replaces DNS-based mechanisms like include and a with their corresponding IP addresses. While this reduces DNS lookups, it can make SPF records harder to maintain.

If an email service provider changes its sending IP addresses, a flattened record may become outdated. Administrators need to update it regularly to help prevent authentication failures.

  • Manual maintenance: Provider infrastructure changes can require frequent IP updates.

  • Record length: Extensive IP lists can exceed DNS TXT record size limits.

  • Configuration complexity: Managing several sending services can become difficult.

  • Authentication risks: Outdated IP addresses can cause legitimate emails to fail SPF checks.





5 Alternatives to SPF Flattening for Managing the 10 DNS Lookup Limit


1. Remove Unnecessary SPF Includes

A simple way to reduce DNS lookups is to review your SPF record and remove unnecessary mechanisms. Over time, businesses may add includes for email marketing platforms, CRMs, helpdesk tools, and other third-party services they no longer use. Removing outdated entries can reduce DNS queries without adding maintenance work. Before deleting an include, confirm that the service no longer sends email on your domain’s behalf to avoid SPF failures for legitimate messages. Remove outdated or unused SPF includes to reduce unnecessary DNS lookups, simplify your SPF record, and avoid authentication errors.


2. Consolidate Email Sending Services

Another way to simplify SPF is to consolidate email services with fewer providers. Organizations may use separate platforms for marketing, transactional messages, customer notifications, and internal communications, and each provider can add SPF DNS lookups. Reviewing sending needs and removing duplicate services can reduce the number of required SPF includes. For example, a business might move its marketing and transactional email services to fewer providers. Consolidation can also make email authentication easier to monitor and maintain.


3. Use Subdomains for Different Email Services

Organizations can assign email-sending services to separate subdomains rather than route them all through one primary domain. For example, marketing.example.com can send promotional emails, support.example.com can handle customer support messages, and alerts.example.com can send automated notifications. Each subdomain can have its own SPF record, so authentication requirements can be managed separately. This setup must be configured correctly for each sending service and its From domain; adding subdomains alone won’t address lookup problems if every service still uses the same SPF identity.


4. Choose Email Providers with Efficient SPF Requirements

Some email providers require multiple DNS lookups for SPF, while others use simpler configurations. Before choosing a provider, review its authentication documentation and check how its SPF mechanisms will affect your existing record. Look for minimal SPF requirements, clear guidance, reliable IP infrastructure, and support for DKIM and DMARC. Choosing carefully can help avoid unnecessary SPF complexity as your business grows. Choose email providers with few SPF DNS lookups and clear authentication guidelines to simplify record management and stay within the 10-lookup limit.





5. Use Automated SPF Management Tools

Automated SPF management offers a practical alternative to maintaining complex records by hand. Tools such as AutoSPF help organizations monitor SPF configurations, manage DNS lookup limits, and keep authentication records up to date. Rather than tracking every sending provider and IP address manually, businesses can use automated monitoring to spot configuration issues. This approach is especially useful for organizations that rely on multiple email platforms or frequently change their sending infrastructure. Automated SPF management tools monitor DNS lookups, keep SPF records up to date, and help prevent authentication errors—with less need for ongoing manual maintenance.


Best Practices for Managing the SPF 10 DNS Lookup Limit


Whichever SPF approach you choose, follow established management practices:

  • Monitor DNS lookups: Check regularly that your SPF record stays within the 10-lookup limit.

  • Use one SPF record: Publish only one SPF record per domain.

  • Review third-party services: Remove unused email providers and outdated includes.

  • Add DKIM and DMARC: Use these complementary authentication protocols to strengthen email security.

  • Audit regularly: Review SPF settings whenever your email infrastructure changes.
