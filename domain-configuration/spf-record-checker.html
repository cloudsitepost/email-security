What Does An SPF Record Checker Reveal
About Your Domain Configuration?


Email security helps protect a domain from spoofing, phishing, and unauthorized email activity. Organizations that send messages through multiple platforms need correctly configured authentication so legitimate emails can reach recipients. One key measure is the Sender Policy Framework (SPF), which specifies the mail servers authorized to send email for a domain. Publishing an SPF record alone, however, does not ensure it is configured correctly. An SPF record checker can review DNS settings, flag configuration issues, and help domain owners assess whether their email authentication policies are working as intended. Using those findings, businesses can work to improve email deliverability and strengthen domain protection. Click here for further details.


What Is an SPF Record Checker?


An SPF record checker examines a domain’s published SPF record in DNS to verify its syntax and flag issues that may affect email authentication.

It reviews the SPF policy and shows which sending sources are authorized, how many DNS lookups are required, and whether the configuration contains errors. This helps administrators assess whether the domain is set up to prevent unauthorized servers from sending email.

For businesses using Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, SendGrid, or marketing automation platforms, regular checks help confirm that legitimate email services are included in the domain’s SPF policy.





What Does an SPF Record Checker Reveal?


1. Published SPF Record and Syntax Errors

An SPF checker locates the TXT record published in a domain’s DNS configuration and verifies that it begins with the correct SPF version, typically `v=spf1`. It also checks that the mechanisms and qualifiers use valid syntax. Common mechanisms include:

  • `ip4` and `ip6`: Authorize specific IPv4 and IPv6 addresses.

  • `include`: Lets another domain’s SPF policy authorize sending servers.

  • `a`: Uses the domain’s A or AAAA records for authorization.

  • `mx`: Authorizes servers listed in the domain’s MX records.

  • `all`: Specifies how other sending sources should be handled.

The checker can flag missing elements, invalid mechanisms, and formatting errors that may cause SPF evaluation failures.


2. Authorized Email Sending Sources

An SPF record checker shows which servers and services are authorized to send email from your domain—a useful check for businesses that rely on multiple providers. For example, a company might use Microsoft 365 for employee communication, SendGrid for transactional messages, and a separate marketing platform for promotional campaigns. If a legitimate sender is missing from the SPF record, its emails may fail SPF authentication. Reviewing the authorized sources helps administrators keep the record accurate and identify services to add or remove.





3. DNS Lookup Limit

 An SPF checker can show how many DNS lookups an SPF record requires. The SPF specification allows up to 10 DNS-triggering mechanisms and modifiers during evaluation. Exceeding that limit can cause a PermError, which may prevent legitimate email from passing authentication. Checkers can identify lookup-consuming mechanisms such as include, a, mx, and exists, helping administrators spot unnecessary nested includes or complex DNS configurations. An SPF optimization solution such as AutoSPF can help flatten records, reduce lookup requirements, and support reliable email authentication.


4. Multiple SPF Records

A hostname should have only one SPF record. If multiple SPF TXT records are published, receiving servers may return a PermError while evaluating the domain’s SPF policy. An SPF checker can help administrators find duplicate records and combine the required authorization mechanisms into one valid record—a useful step when departments or service providers manage DNS independently.


5. SPF Policy and Qualifiers

An SPF checker also shows how a policy handles mail from sources it doesn’t explicitly authorize. The policy’s final mechanism tells receiving servers how to interpret those senders:

  • `+all`: Permits all sending sources and generally poses serious security risks.

  • `~all`: Marks unauthorized sources as soft failures.

  • `-all`: Indicates that unauthorized sources should fail SPF authentication.

  • `?all`: Applies a neutral result.

Understanding these qualifiers helps administrators assess whether their SPF policy offers an appropriate level of protection.


Why Should Businesses Check Their SPF Records Regularly?


SPF records can become outdated when you change email providers, add marketing platforms, or migrate business infrastructure. Regular SPF checks help identify unauthorized or outdated senders, catch configuration errors, stay within the 10-DNS-lookup limit, improve authentication reliability, reduce domain-spoofing risk, and support email deliverability.

Pairing SPF monitoring with DKIM and DMARC provides stronger protection against email-based threats. Regularly review SPF records to catch configuration errors, remove outdated sending sources, and stay within the 10-DNS-lookup limit. Ongoing monitoring can also help prevent email spoofing, strengthen domain security, and support reliable email delivery.
