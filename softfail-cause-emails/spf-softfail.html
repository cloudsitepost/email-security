Can SPF Softfail Cause Emails
To Land In Spam?

Email deliverability is essential to effective communication. Whether a business sends marketing campaigns, transactional notifications, customer updates, or internal messages, its emails need to reach recipients’ inboxes instead of being filtered as spam. The Sender Policy Framework (SPF) authentication result is one factor that can affect deliverability.

SPF helps receiving mail servers confirm whether a message came from a server authorized by the sending domain. If a message fails SPF, the receiving server may treat it as suspicious, but failure doesn’t always mean automatic rejection. A softfail means the sending server isn’t explicitly authorized by the domain’s SPF record, while the domain owner has opted not to require strict rejection.

This raises an important question: Can SPF softfail cause emails to land in spam?

Yes. SPF softfail can affect spam filtering and email deliverability, but it doesn’t automatically send a message to spam. Mailbox providers weigh multiple authentication and reputation signals when deciding where to deliver email. Knowing what SPF softfail means, why it happens, and how to resolve it can help organizations protect their domains and maintain reliable delivery.





What Is SPF Softfail?


An SPF softfail means the sending server isn’t authorized by the domain’s SPF record, but the domain owner hasn’t asked receiving servers to reject the message outright.

An SPF record is a DNS TXT record that identifies which mail servers and IP addresses are permitted to send emails on behalf of a domain. When an email arrives, the receiving mail server checks the sender's domain and evaluates the sending server's IP address against the published SPF record.

If the sending server does not match an authorized source and the SPF record ends with the ~all qualifier, the receiving server may return an SPF softfail result.

For example:

v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all

In this example, the SPF record authorizes servers listed in Google's SPF infrastructure. The ~all mechanism indicates that other sending servers should receive a softfail result.

Unlike -all, which represents a hardfail, ~all communicates that unauthorized sources should be treated with suspicion rather than explicitly rejected by the SPF policy.


How SPF Softfail Differs From SPF Hardfail

SPF softfail and hardfail both signal that a sending server isn’t authorized by the domain’s SPF record. The difference is how strongly the domain states its policy. With softfail (~all), the domain indicates that the sender is probably unauthorized, which receiving servers can consider when evaluating the message. With hardfail (-all), the domain explicitly says that other sending sources aren’t authorized—a stronger signal receiving servers can use when deciding whether to reject or filter the email.

For example:

SPF softfail:

v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all

SPF hardfail:

v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com -all

Neither qualifier guarantees a particular inbox placement or rejection outcome. The receiving provider makes the final delivery decision based on its own policies and other authentication and reputation signals.





Can SPF Softfail Cause Emails To Land In Spam?


Yes, SPF softfail can contribute to emails landing in spam folders. When a receiving mail server detects a softfail result, it knows that the sending IP address is not explicitly authorized by the domain's SPF policy.

Although softfail does not automatically trigger rejection, it can negatively influence the receiving server's assessment of the message.

Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, and other mailbox providers consider several factors when deciding whether to deliver an email to the inbox, send it to spam, or reject it. These include SPF authentication, DKIM signatures, DMARC alignment, sending IP and domain reputation, message content, and recipient engagement.

If an email receives an SPF softfail result and also has other authentication problems, the possibility of spam filtering may increase.


Why Receiving Servers Consider SPF Softfail Suspicious

SPF is designed to help prevent unauthorized email servers from sending messages using another domain's identity.

When an email receives a softfail result, the receiving server cannot confirm that the sending IP address is authorized under the domain's SPF policy.

This may indicate several possible problems:

  • The email is being sent from an unauthorized mail server.

  • The domain's SPF record does not include all legitimate sending services.

  • A third-party email marketing platform has not been properly configured.

  • An attacker may be attempting to impersonate the domain.

  • The SPF record contains outdated or incorrect authorization information.
